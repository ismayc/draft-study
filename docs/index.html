Draft model: does college production beat the scouts?

Question. The draft pick is the scouting industry's consensus forecast. Given a player's final college season, does box-score production predict NBA outcomes beyond what the pick already encodes?

Terms used in this analysis. Dotted-underlined terms anywhere below repeat these definitions on hover (full glossary).

  • IRLS — Iteratively reweighted least squares - the standard algorithm for fitting a logistic regression.
  • AUC — Area under the ROC curve: the probability the model ranks a random positive case above a random negative one. 0.5 is a coin flip.

Data

Source What How
barttorvik.com advanced stats (the cbbdata/toRvik upstream) 56,781 college player-seasons, 2011-2022, 67 columns free CSV endpoint, no key; header pinned empirically and gated
nba_api DraftHistory draft year, pick, NBA PERSON_ID one call
nba_api LeagueDashPlayerStats NBA minutes, 2011-12 .. 2024-25 (the outcome) 14 cached calls

NBA minutes join by ID, not name; the one name join in the study (Torvik ↔ DraftHistory) is gated at 98%+ with every historical mismatch audited into an explicit nickname/name-change alias table (Bam Adebayo, Mo Bamba, Bones Hyland, ...).

Design

  • Sample: drafted players whose final college season is 2011-2022 (n=567).
  • Outcome: ≥2,000 NBA minutes within three seasons of the draft.
  • Models: logistic M0: log(pick) vs M1: log(pick) + 15 college features (age, height, OBPM/DBPM, usage, TS%, rebound/assist/turnover/stock rates, 3PA volume, FT rate, class), fit by the same hand-rolled IRLS as shot-quality-study, mirrored in R.
  • Era-based holdout: train on classes 2011-2018, evaluate on 2019-2022. Random splits leak era effects; time splits are how a front office would actually use the model.

Pipeline

Validation gates (output/validation.csv): draft-year inference agrees with DraftHistory ≥97%; college lottery picks matched ≥98% (the two known exceptions never played a college minute); outcome base rate in a sane band; pick-only test AUC ≥ 0.65.

Findings

Every number below is generated by python/04_findings.py from output/, not typed by hand. Both implementations (R and Python) reconcile to numeric tolerance (observed ~1e-13) before this is written.

The setup

567 drafted college players, classes 2011-2022, outcome = 2,000+ NBA minutes within three seasons of the draft (base rate 54%). Train: classes 2011-2018. Test: classes 2019-2022 — drafts the model never saw.

The headline is a negative result, reported as one

model train AUC test AUC train log-loss test log-loss
pick only 0.844 0.827 0.497 0.528
pick + college stats 0.861 0.827 0.470 0.538

In sample, college production looks useful (+0.017 AUC). Out of sample the advantage is +0.000 AUC and log-loss actually degrades — the textbook signature of overfitting, shown on real data. The draft pick is the scouting industry's aggregate forecast, and on this outcome, at this sample size, box-score college production adds nothing the scouts had not already priced in. That is the finding. A junior analyst's version of this study ends with the train-set table and a claim that college stats "improve the model by two AUC points"; the era-based holdout is the difference between that story and the truth.

What it does NOT say: that college stats are useless (richer outcomes, bigger samples, or interactions may yet pay), or that scouting is unbeatable — public models like DARKO-derived draft work use far richer inputs. It says the cheap version of the claim fails honestly.

What a draft slot is actually worth

The pick-only curve is itself decision-relevant (see figures/fig1_pick_curve_py.html): P(rotation player) falls from 99.9% at pick 1 (an extrapolation to the curve's edge — read it as "nearly certain") to a coin flip around pick 28, and keeps falling — draft capital is front-loaded exactly the way trade valuations assume.

Individual residuals are anecdotes, not evidence; they are listed because a front office will always ask.

Honest limitations

  • Binary outcome and a blunt one; minutes confound opportunity with merit.
  • College-only: international and G-League-Ignite paths are out of frame, and two lottery picks (Sharpe, Kanter) never logged a college minute.
  • 567 players is small; the negative result is about THIS feature set at THIS sample size, not a theorem about college data.
  • Name matching is gated (98.8% draft-year agreement; every mismatch was audited by hand into an explicit alias table in the code).
